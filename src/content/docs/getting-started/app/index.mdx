## What an app is

<FlowStrip steps="A table in Storage|A small web app that runs inside Keboola and reads it|A URL you send to whoever you choose" label="What an app is" />

A grid, a dashboard or an explorer. Like everything else in this guide it is a component, so it can
sit in a flow; the [Apps section](/data-apps/) covers it in full.

## Set it up

<Tabs syncKey="howto">
<TabItem label="Prompt">

Describing an app is a one-paragraph job, and the Apps section has Kai wired in as the primary way
to build one ([Build your first app with Kai](/data-apps/getting-started/)).

1. Open **Apps** in the navigation. In a project with no apps yet, that page is the builder itself:
   **Build web apps from your Keboola data** and a field that says *Describe what you want to
   build...*. Once the project has an app, the page lists it, with a **Create app** button for the
   next one. The ways to bring your own code sit below the field; the **UI** tab says where they
   are covered.

2. Describe the app and send it:

   ```text title="Prompt" wrap
   Build a data app from the table out.c-Boolabean-staffing-outlook.staffing_outlook. Show one grid: a row per café (store_name and city) and a column per forecast date, in date order. Each cell shows expected_units_per_person as the big number, with the weekday, forecast_temp_c and forecast_rain_mm in small text underneath. Colour a cell red when short_handed is true and leave the other cells neutral. Above the grid, one caption line: how many café-days in the table are short-handed. No filters, no other charts, no sidebar.
   ```

   Saying what *not* to build is half the prompt: left to itself Kai adds filters and a second
   chart. Kai also names the app, *Staffing Outlook* in our run; the **Apps** list shows what it
   called yours.

3. Kai builds a **draft** of the app without asking first: it creates the app, writes the code and
   starts a live preview, and nothing is published until you say so. The builder shows the chat on
   the left and **Preview** and **Code** on the right; when it is done, the chat ends with **Draft
   ready**, *Not live yet*. Expect about five minutes to a grid you can look at. Not what you
   wanted? Say so in the same chat, and the draft updates.

   ![Screenshot - The Apps builder right after the prompt is sent: the prompt in the chat on the left with Kai thinking, and on the right a New App with Preview and Code tabs and an empty pane that says the app will appear there](/getting-started/app/kai-builder.png)

4. Check the preview itself: six rows, seven columns, one big number per cell, red where `short_handed` is true, and the caption's count matching the red cells. If it
   does not match, say so in the same chat; the draft updates in place. In our Free Plan run the
   first preview had no red cells and a caption saying none were short-handed, while Kai's summary
   said three; one message in the same chat, naming the three rows, fixed it (the fold under
   **If it goes wrong** has one to copy). Ours, on a week with three red days:

   ![Screenshot - The grid in the draft preview: six cafés by seven days from 1 to 7 October, three red cells (Vinohrady on Thursday, Zelný trh on Monday and Tuesday) under the caption 3 short-handed café-days in this outlook](/getting-started/app/grid-preview.png)

   {/* Draft preview iframe captured 2026-10-01 in Free Plan project 6375 at 2x, after the
   one-message fix; replaces the 264 capture of 2026-09-14 (two red cells, another week). Same week
   as the run's other screenshots. */}

5. **Optional, to share it:** under **What's next?** in the chat, click **Publish to production**.
   Kai moves the draft's code into the app and asks twice, the first approvals on this page: once
   to give the published app access to Storage, and once to deploy it. A few minutes later the
   app's own page (**Apps**, then the app) says **Active** and offers **Open App**, and the grid
   sits behind the password from the app's **Authentication** setting, **Basic (Password)** by
   default. This worked in our Free Plan project; in an older project the deploy failed, for the
   reasons below. The builder also shows **Deploy** buttons, in its header and on the *Draft ready*
   card; this guide uses the **Publish to production** chip. The draft preview is enough to finish
   it. To remove the app later, open **More actions** (**⋯**) on its page and pick **Delete app**.

**Check:** the draft preview (or **Open App**, once deployed) shows the grid, and its caption's
count equals both the number of red cells and the number of rows where `short_handed` is true in
Storage. The count moves with the forecast. The sample's history is one summer, so a week under
18 °C has no red cells at all, and on cold, dry days most cafés have no figure to show; that is the
right answer for such a week, and the fold on red cells below says how to test the colouring
anyway.

While the builder chat is open, also ask Kai:
`Does this app read staffing_outlook on every request, or only when it starts?` The weekly flow on
the next page rebuilds the table but does not restart the app. Ours reads it on every request. An
app that reads it only when it starts still shows a new week once it has slept and started again.
To be sure, ask Kai in the same chat to make yours read the table on every request. If the app is
already published, click **Publish to production** again so the live app gets the change.

</TabItem>

<TabItem label="CLI / API">

The app itself is written by Kai in the builder. No command turns a paragraph into a grid.
What `kbagent` does is the part you repeat: listing apps, deploying the version Storage
holds, waking one that has gone to sleep, and reading its log when it will not start.

```bash
kbagent data-app list --project docs-demo
```

That prints each app with its numeric **app ID**, its state and its URL. `kbagent data-app detail
--project docs-demo --app-id <app-id>` adds the Storage **configuration ID**, a different
identifier.

With the app ID, a deploy of the latest saved configuration is one command:

```bash
kbagent data-app deploy --project docs-demo --app-id <app-id> --wait
```

`--wait` blocks until the container is running or the deploy fails.

Four more worth knowing:

```bash
kbagent data-app start --project docs-demo --app-id <app-id>
```

```bash
kbagent data-app logs --project docs-demo --app-id <app-id>
```

```bash
kbagent data-app runs --project docs-demo --app-id <app-id>
```

```bash
kbagent data-app password --project docs-demo --app-id <app-id>
```

`start` wakes an app that auto-suspended, `logs` is the app's own output, `runs` lists each
start with the container's startup log, which is where a failed deploy actually explains itself,
and `password` prints the Basic-auth password for the URL, the one thing to keep out of a
screenshot or a chat.

</TabItem>

## If it goes wrong

<details>
<summary>The grid is empty, or shows another table</summary>

On a cold, dry day most cells have no figure by design: under 18 °C with no rain, five of the six
cafés have no summer history to average ([Transform](/getting-started/transform/) explains). If the
whole grid is empty or shows other data, the app is reading the wrong table. Give Kai the full
table ID, `out.c-Boolabean-staffing-outlook.staffing_outlook`, in the same chat and let the draft
update.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The red cells do not match the caption</summary>

Kai coloured on a threshold of its own instead of the `short_handed` column. Tell it to use the
column; the transformation already decided what red means, and the app should not decide again.

</details>

<details>
<summary>No cell is red, and the caption says none are short-handed</summary>

If Storage has no rows where `short_handed` is true, the grid is right: a week under 18 °C flags
nothing. If it has some, the app read the column wrong. In our Free Plan run it reached the app as
`1` or an empty string rather than true or false, so nothing matched, while Kai's own summary in
the chat still said three cells were red. Say what you see in the same chat, for example like this
(ours also named the three rows):

```text title="Prompt" wrap
The preview shows no red cells, but staffing_outlook has rows where short_handed is true. Check how short_handed arrives in the app, then colour those cells red and make the caption count them.
```

In a week with nothing flagged you can still test the colouring: ask Kai in the same chat how the
app reads `short_handed`. It should turn whatever arrives (in our run the text `1` or an empty
string) into true or false before it colours a cell.

</details>

<details>
<summary>The production deploy fails, and Terminal Logs says the app is not running</summary>

The draft ran, so the code works; the production container did not come up. The job's own error is
usually just *Internal Server Error occurred*, which says nothing. The reason is in the container's
startup log, and the fastest way to read it is
`kbagent data-app runs --project <alias> --app-id <app-id>`. Two causes have shown up in our older
project, and neither is your grid (the same deploy worked in a new Free Plan project):

- `App must have keboola-config/nginx/ directory`: the repository Kai wrote has no nginx
  configuration, which a Python/JS app needs in production. The draft does not, because it runs in
  dev mode.
- `Authentication failed for https://git.<stack>/keboola/app-<id>.git` with *Credentials are
  incorrect or have expired*: the app's managed Git credentials have expired, so the container
  cannot clone its own code.

After several failures the platform disables the app: a banner says it failed to start
automatically multiple times, and the app's page offers **Start**. Either message is one for
[Support](/management/support/), with the run ID from the command above. The rest of the pipeline
is unaffected: the table is rebuilt every week whether or not the app is up, and the draft
preview still shows it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Nothing happens for a minute after you open the app</summary>

It went to sleep. Apps stop after a while without visitors (**Auto Sleep** on the app's page; ours
was set to fifteen minutes) and wake on the next request, which is the wait you are seeing. While
it sleeps, the Apps list and the app's page say **Stopped**, and the page offers **Start**.

</details>

## Going further

- [Publish and share](/data-apps/publish-and-share/): authentication options beyond the default
  password, and who can reach the URL.
- [Authentication](/data-apps/authentication/): who can open the app. **Basic (Password)** is the
  default. Every other gate (OIDC with Auth0, Google Cloud, Microsoft Entra ID or Okta, or GitHub,
  GitLab and JumpCloud) needs a client you register in that provider's own console, which neither
  Kai nor `kbagent` does for you; `kbagent data-app create --auth` offers only `password` or
  `public`.
- [Build locally](/data-apps/build-locally/): the Git-based workflow once an app outgrows its
  first description.
