A small café chain writes its rosters the way most small businesses do: by the day of the week.
Two people on Monday and Tuesday, three the rest of the week. Demand does not read the roster. It
reads the weather, so a hot, dry Tuesday outsells a wet Saturday, and the two people behind the
counter find out at eleven in the morning.

The question this guide answers:

> **Which day next week will leave a café short-handed?**

You have the data to answer it: a summer of sales, the rosters that served it, and the weather
each day, spread across five files, none of which answers alone. The sixth piece does not exist
yet. Next week's weather comes from a public forecast API that needs no account, and it is the
reason the answer changes every morning.

In under an hour you will have a table that answers the question for six cafés and seven days, an
app anyone can open to read it, and a flow that rebuilds both before the first café opens. It is a
working, scheduled pipeline rather than an exercise. Most steps are one Kai prompt: copy it, watch
it build, check the result. Every step also has the click-through version. Everything happens in
the browser, nothing needs installing, and a
[free project](https://connection.us-east4.gcp.keboola.com/wizard) covers all of it without a
sales call.

## What you will build

| Phase | What happens | Keboola calls it |
|---|---|---|
| Load | five CSV files and a live forecast, fetched from URLs, become six tables | a **data source connector** |
| Transform | SQL joins them into one table: a café, a day, the demand to expect, the crew rostered, a flag | a **transformation** |
| Ask | the question, answered out of that table in plain words | **Kai** reading your data |
| Publish | a grid of six cafés by seven days, red where it will hurt | a **data app** |
| Automate | all of it re-runs every morning on the new forecast, and tells you if it breaks | a **flow** |
| Check | what should be in Storage and Jobs, and the failures that actually happen | — |

The sales file alone can plot a summer. It cannot tell you what any of it *means*: it names cafés
and products by ID, it knows nothing about who was on shift, and it has no idea what the weather
was. Four small tables (the cafés, the menu, the daily roster, the daily weather) hold the missing
halves, and the forecast turns a history into a warning. Joining them is the whole trick.

The joined table is the one a manager would read: one row per café per forecast day, with the
temperature and rain expected, the demand that kind of day has produced before, the crew rostered
for that weekday, and how many cups each of them would have to sell. Cross a line and the row is
flagged. Some weeks nobody crosses it, because a cool, wet forecast is a quiet one, and the grid
says so. A quiet week is still an answer.

2–4 people on shift, 552 weather rows, and a 42-row live forecast (6 × 7). Values on the day of

## Get a project

Everything you build lives inside a **project**: the data you load, the SQL you write, the flow
that runs them. Kai works *inside* a project, so getting one is the one step it cannot do for you.
It comes first, and it is yours.

Three ways in, in order of how quickly they get you started:

1. **Free Plan**: [sign up and create a free project](https://connection.us-east4.gcp.keboola.com/wizard)
   right now. It is enough for everything in this guide: 250 GB of Storage and up to 60 minutes
   of job runtime a month (the free allowance tracks your previous month's usage).

   {/* VERIFY(owner): /management/payg-project/ says the monthly allowance is "limited to the
   actual usage from the previous month" but never states what a project gets in its first month.
   Worth stating on that page. */} See [Pay As You Go](/management/payg-project/) for the full
   list of limits, including that the Free Plan does not let you choose a stack and gives you one
   project.
2. **Join an existing project**: ask an admin of your company's Keboola organization to invite
   you. They do it from **Users & Settings** in the project, with **Invite User**.
3. **Talk to us**: for a trial of a full project with a chosen stack and no Free Plan limits,
   [contact Keboola](https://www.keboola.com/contact).

A project is a self-contained workspace with its own data storage, configurations, users and
billing. Nothing leaks between projects unless you deliberately share it through the
[Data Catalog](/catalog/). Each project lives on one **stack**, a full, independent instance of
Keboola in one cloud provider and one region, such as `connection.eu-central-1.keboola.com` (AWS
Frankfurt). Projects never span stacks, and an account on one stack does not exist on another, so
the URL you sign in at is part of your project's identity. You do not pick a stack here: self-serve
projects are created on a fixed one, and any other stack comes through a sales contract, the
**Talk to us** route above. The list of stacks is in the [platform overview](/overview/#stacks).
Larger organizations run several projects;
[Multi-Project Architecture](/overview/onboarding/architecture-guide/) covers that when you get to
it.

### Find your way around

After signing in you land on the project dashboard. Five places matter for this guide:

- **Components** holds every data source connector, data destination connector, and application
  you can add to the project. This is where you configure how data gets in and out.
- **Transformations** is the code that reshapes your data: SQL, Python, R, and dbt. Which of them a
  project offers is listed in its own **Create Transformation** dialog, so that dialog is the
  answer to "what can this project run?".
- **Storage** lists every table and file in the project, organized into buckets. Its tabs cover
  **Tables & Buckets**, **Files**, **Data Streams**, **Storage Jobs** and **Events**.
- **Flows** is the automation layer: what runs, in what order, on what schedule. Projects that have
  both kinds show this as **Conditional Flows**.
- **Jobs** is the log of everything that has run, successfully or not.

**Check it worked.** Open **Storage** and you can see the project's bucket list. Empty is fine;
that is what you are about to change. **Kai Agent** opens a chat panel when you click it. If the
button is missing, or clicking it asks you to switch the feature on, that is the feature gate: an
organization admin does it, and a non-admin needs them or Support. There is also a monthly message
allowance, and it is smaller on Pay As You Go.

**If it goes wrong.** The sign-up email never arrives: check spam, then try again. A half-finished
registration does not block a retry with the same address. You sign in and see no project: you are
on the wrong stack, or your invite is still pending, so confirm the exact URL with whoever invited
you. Your company uses SSO: sign in through your organization's link rather than creating a new
account, because a fresh Free Plan project will not have access to your team's data.

## Two ways to do every step

Every building page opens with a **Do it with Kai** tab (copy the prompt, watch it build, check the
result) and a **Do it yourself** tab with every click written out, for when you want to see the
mechanics. The project above is the one thing that had to be yours; the Google authorization on
the spreadsheet side trip is the other.

Kai asks before it changes anything: expect one approval dialog per object it creates, while
questions that only read do not ask. If confirming each one gets tiring, **Always allow** on a
dialog and [tool permissions](/kai/settings/#tool-permissions) pre-approve a tool for the rest of
the chat or the project. The chat's **plan mode** button is a different thing. Kai writes the steps
down and asks you to approve the plan before it starts, and then still asks for each change. Use
it when you want to read the plan first.

Not having Kai does not block the build. The loading, the SQL and the flow each carry a

[the same request works as one prompt](/getting-started/ask/#the-same-request-as-one-prompt),

## The steps

1. **[Get your data in](/getting-started/load/)** pulls the five files and the live forecast into
   Storage with a connector, and explains buckets, tables and stages. Two Kai prompts, runs
   included.
2. **[Transform data](/getting-started/transform/)** writes the SQL that puts each forecast day
   next to the demand that kind of weather has produced before, and the crew on that weekday's
   roster. One Kai prompt builds and runs it.
3. **[Ask a question and get an answer](/getting-started/ask/)** puts the guide's question to your
   own data and has Kai answer it out of the table you just built.
4. **[Put it in front of someone](/getting-started/app/)** turns the table into the six-by-seven
   grid, described to Kai in one paragraph and published from the builder.
5. **[Make it run every morning](/getting-started/automate/)** runs the whole thing in order, on a
   schedule, with notifications. Kai wires the flow and, if you ask, the schedule; notifications
   are two clicks.
6. **[Check it worked](/getting-started/check/)** says what should be in Storage and Jobs, gives
   the one number that proves the join, and lists the failures that actually happen.

Read them in order, or land on any one of them: each states where you should be, what it needs,
and how to tell it worked. The **Next** control at the foot of every page follows this order.

## Going further

Optional side trips, once the main path makes sense. None of them are needed to finish the
guide:

- **[Send a table to Google Sheets](/getting-started/write/)** delivers the result to where
  people actually read it. It needs a Google account you can authorize.
- **[Load from Google Sheets](/getting-started/load/googlesheets/)** and
  **[Load from a database](/getting-started/load/database/)** load from a source that needs
  credentials, rather than from the public URLs the guide uses.
- **[Use a workspace](/getting-started/transform/workspace/)** is where you develop and test SQL
  against a copy of your data before committing it to a transformation.
- **[Ad-hoc data analysis](/getting-started/ad-hoc/)** explores arbitrary data in a Python or R
  notebook rather than building a pipeline.
- **[Development Branches](/getting-started/branches/)** changes a running project safely: make
  the change, review the diff, then merge.

## If you are planning a rollout, not learning the tool

This guide is for one person building one pipeline. For introducing Keboola to a team, with
project architecture, a data model, naming conventions and governance, start with
[Platform Onboarding](/overview/onboarding/) instead.
